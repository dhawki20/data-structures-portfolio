What is Data Science to Me?
Using information to find patterns, answer questions and help decision-making.
Write your reflection here: 
Before starting this course, I thought of data science as working with numbers, graphs, and stats. I did not really think about how much of that data actually comes from real people and their everyday lives. When data is connected to people, it becomes more than just numbers on a screen. It can represent someone’s background, behavior, health, education, or performance. That is why I think it is important to learn data science from both a social and interdisciplinary point of view. There are many reasons why we might use data to model social topics and people. It can help us notice patterns that would be hard to see on our own. Like researchers can use data to learn about problems in education, health, employment, and different communities. Since I am interested in sports analytics, I also think about how teams use data to evaluate players, improve performance, and make decisions. Data can be extremely helpful in these situations, but it does not tell us everything. A person cannot be completely understood through a spreadsheet or a statistic. There are also a lot of responsibilities that come with using information about people. Privacy is one of the biggest ones. People should know how their information is being collected and used. We all also have to be careful about bias because a data set might not represent everyone fairly. If the original data is biased or incomplete, the results could lead to unfair conclusions. I think it is important for anyone working with data to be honest about where the information came from and what the results can and cannot prove. Throughout this course, I hope to learn how to use data to better understand real-world issues especially in the sports world. I especially want to learn how to find useful patterns without forgetting that real people are behind the numbers. I also want to become better at deciding whether data is trustworthy and explaining results in a way that makes sense. For my future in sports analytics, I think this will help me use statistics as a helpful tool while still understanding the importance of human and judgment.
